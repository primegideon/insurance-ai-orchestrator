Underwriting Policy Manual — Life Insurance Division
Internal Reference: UPM-2024 Rev 4.1
Classification: Internal Use Only

===========================================================================
SECTION 1 — FINANCIAL ANTI-SELECTION RULES
===========================================================================

Rule 10A — Claim-to-Income Ratio Threshold:
Any claim where the claimed benefit amount exceeds five times (5×) the
policyholder's declared annual income must be flagged for Financial
Anti-Selection. The underwriter must escalate the claim to a Senior
Underwriter for secondary review. A claim-to-income ratio above 5× is a
primary actuarial indicator that the policy was obtained with intent to
over-insure, which constitutes anti-selection fraud.

Rule 10B — Extreme Claim-to-Income Ratio:
Where the claim-to-income ratio exceeds ten times (10×) the policyholder's
declared annual income, the recommendation shall be automatic Rejection
pending a formal fraud investigation. The fraud investigation team must be
notified within 48 hours of flagging. All supporting financial documents
must be requested from the claimant before any benefit disbursement.

Rule 10C — Income Verification Requirement:
For any claim exceeding $250,000, the underwriting team must independently
verify the policyholder's declared annual income against tax records or
employer confirmation letters submitted at policy inception. Discrepancies
of more than 20% between declared and verified income must be treated as
material misrepresentation and escalated accordingly.

SECTION 2 — EARLY CLAIM RULES
===========================================================================

Rule 20A — Early Claim Escalation (0–12 months):
Any claim submitted where the months since policy inception is fewer than
twelve (12) months shall be automatically escalated to a Senior Underwriter,
regardless of claim amount, diagnosis code, or policyholder age. The early
inception period is the highest-risk window for fraudulent policy acquisition.
This rule applies without exception.

Rule 20B — Early Claim Enhanced Due Diligence (12–24 months):
Claims filed between twelve (12) and twenty-four (24) months of policy
inception require enhanced due diligence. The underwriter must verify that
all declared medical history flags at onboarding are consistent with the
current diagnosis. Any inconsistency triggers a material misrepresentation
investigation under Section 3.

Rule 20C — Suicide and Self-Harm Exclusion Clause:
Claims filed within the first twenty-four (24) months of policy inception
where the cause of claim involves self-harm or suicide are excluded from
benefit payment under the standard suicide clause. A formal exclusion notice
must be issued to the claimant within ten (10) business days.

Rule 20D — Policy Lapse and Reinstatement:
If a policy was lapsed and subsequently reinstated within the twelve (12)
months prior to claim submission, the inception date for all early-claim
rules shall be reset to the reinstatement date, not the original inception
date. Underwriters must verify reinstatement history before processing any
claim to ensure the correct inception reference is used.

SECTION 3 — MATERIAL MISREPRESENTATION RULES
===========================================================================

Rule 30A — Definition of Material Misrepresentation:
Material misrepresentation occurs when a policyholder knowingly or
negligently omits or misstates a material fact during the application
process, including medical history, pre-existing conditions, lifestyle
risk factors, or financial information, and where such omission or
misstatement would have affected the underwriter's decision to issue
the policy or the premium rate applied.

Rule 30B — Undisclosed Pre-Existing Conditions:
Where a claim diagnosis code corresponds to a condition (e.g. cardiac
disease, diabetes, chronic respiratory illness, cancer) that was not
declared as a medical history flag at policy application, the claim
must be suspended and referred to the Medical Investigation Unit. The
underwriter shall recommend Rejection if the investigation confirms the
condition was present or clinically diagnosable at the time of application.

Rule 30C — Cardiac Diagnosis Misrepresentation:
Any claim containing a diagnosis code for acute myocardial infarction
(I21.9), heart failure (I50.9), or ischemic heart disease (I25.9) where
the policyholder's medical history flags at onboarding contain no mention
of cardiac or cardiovascular conditions must be treated as a potential
material misrepresentation and escalated immediately.

Rule 30D — Cancer and Malignancy Disclosure:
Claims involving malignant neoplasm codes (C00–C97) where no prior cancer
history or oncology treatment was declared at inception are subject to an
automatic 90-day investigation hold. Benefit payment is suspended until
the Medical Investigation Unit completes its review.

Rule 30E — Diabetes and Metabolic Conditions:
Where a claim involves diabetes-related complications (E10–E14) and the
policyholder declared no metabolic or endocrine conditions at onboarding,
the claim requires enhanced scrutiny. The underwriter must request the
policyholder's general practitioner records for the three-year period
prior to policy inception.

Rule 30F — Contesting a Policy for Misrepresentation:
Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, a life insurance policy may be
contested within two (2) years of inception on the grounds of material
misrepresentation. After two years, the policy becomes incontestable
except in cases of outright fraud. Underwriters must note the inception
date when applying this rule.

SECTION 4 — HIGH-RISK TRIANGLE RULES
===========================================================================

Rule 40A — The High-Risk Triangle Definition:
The High-Risk Triangle is the simultaneous co-occurrence of three actuarial
risk factors: (1) policyholder age greater than sixty-five (65) years,
(2) policy tenure less than thirty-six (36) months since inception, and
(3) a claim benefit amount exceeding one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000).
When all three conditions are present simultaneously, the claim must be
flagged for Anti-Selection Risk and referred to the Fraud Investigation
Department for mandatory secondary manual review.

Rule 40B — High-Risk Triangle — Escalation Procedure:
Upon identification of a High-Risk Triangle claim, the handling underwriter
must: (a) suspend benefit disbursement immediately, (b) notify the Fraud
Investigation Department within 24 hours, (c) request the policyholder's
complete medical records from the past five years, and (d) obtain independent
actuarial verification of the declared annual income.

Rule 40C — High-Risk Triangle — Partial Conditions:
Where only two of the three High-Risk Triangle conditions are present, the
claim shall be escalated to a Senior Underwriter rather than the Fraud
Department. The Senior Underwriter has discretion to approve, escalate
further, or reject based on the totality of the claim evidence.

Rule 40D — Age-Based Risk Loading:
For all policyholders aged sixty-five (65) or above, claims must be reviewed
with heightened actuarial scrutiny. Late-age policy acquisition combined with
any one additional risk factor (early claim, high claim amount, undisclosed
condition) is sufficient grounds for escalation. Actuarial tables show that
the probability of a fraudulent late-age policy acquisition is 4.7× higher
than the base population average.

SECTION 5 — FRAUD DETECTION AND INVESTIGATION PROCEDURES
===========================================================================

Rule 50A — Composite Fraud Indicator:
When a claim triggers two or more independent fraud indicators simultaneously
(e.g. Early Claim + High Claim-to-Income Ratio, or High-Risk Triangle +
Undisclosed Condition), the compounding effect materially increases fraud
probability. Such compound-flagged claims must receive an automatic
Rejection recommendation pending a full fraud investigation, overriding
any single-factor approval logic.

Rule 50B — Referral to Fraud Investigation Unit:
All claims flagged under Rules 10B, 20A, 40A, 40B, or 50A must be formally
referred to the Fraud Investigation Unit (FIU) with a complete case file
including: original application form, claim submission form, all supporting
medical documentation, policy schedule, and the underwriter's written
assessment of the specific fraud indicators identified.

Rule 50C — Benefit Suspension During Investigation:
No benefit disbursement shall occur while a claim is under active fraud
investigation. Claimants must be notified in writing within five (5)
business days of investigation commencement. The FIU has a maximum of
ninety (90) days to complete its investigation, after which a definitive
determination must be issued.

Rule 50D — Suspicious Activity Reporting:
Where the FIU concludes that a claim involves deliberate fraud, the case
must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Centre in accordance with
applicable anti-money laundering and insurance fraud statutes. Policy
cancellation with forfeiture of all premiums paid is the mandatory outcome
for confirmed fraud cases.

SECTION 6 — STANDARD APPROVAL CRITERIA
===========================================================================

Rule 60A — Low-Risk Claim Approval:
A claim may be approved without escalation where all of the following
conditions are met: (a) the claim-to-income ratio does not exceed 3×,
(b) the months since policy inception exceeds 36 months, (c) the diagnosis
codes are consistent with declared medical history flags at onboarding,
(d) the policyholder's age does not exceed 65 years, and (e) no prior
claims have been submitted under this policy within the past 24 months.

Rule 60B — Benefit Payment Timeline:
Approved claims must be settled within thirty (30) calendar days of the
approval decision. The Claims Processing Unit is responsible for initiating
bank transfer or cheque issuance within five (5) business days of the
underwriter's written approval.

Rule 60C — Partial Benefit Approval:
In cases where a claim is partially valid but contains elements subject to
exclusion or investigation, the underwriter may approve the undisputed
portion of the benefit and escalate the disputed portion separately. The
claimant must be informed in writing of any partial approval and the grounds
for the disputed portion's suspension.

SECTION 7 — DIAGNOSIS CODE REFERENCE FOR UNDERWRITERS
===========================================================================

The following ICD-10 diagnosis codes represent high-scrutiny categories
requiring heightened underwriter attention under Sections 3, 4, and 5:

Cardiac Conditions (Rule 30C applies):
  I21.9 — Acute myocardial infarction, unspecified
  I50.9 — Heart failure, unspecified
  I25.9 — Chronic ischemic heart disease, unspecified
  I10   — Essential primary hypertension (requires history verification)

Respiratory Conditions:
  J45   — Asthma (Rule 42B: if not declared at onboarding, reject for misrepresentation)
  J44.1 — COPD with acute exacerbation
  J18.9 — Pneumonia, unspecified organism

Metabolic Conditions (Rule 30E applies):
  E11.9 — Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications
  E10.9 — Type 1 diabetes mellitus without complications
  E66.9 — Obesity, unspecified

Malignant Conditions (Rule 30D applies):
  C34.9 — Malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung, unspecified
  C50.9 — Malignant neoplasm of breast, unspecified
  C61   — Malignant neoplasm of prostate

Neurological Conditions:
  G35   — Multiple sclerosis
  I63.9 — Cerebral infarction, unspecified (stroke)
  G20   — Parkinson's disease

For all codes in the above categories: if the condition was not declared
as a medical history flag at policy application, apply Rule 30B immediately.
